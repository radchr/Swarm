## Small example: eigenvectors

Learning result: Given a transformation that stretches horizontally and leaves vertical distances unchanged, the viewer can identify arrows that retain their line and predict the effect of changing the stretch factor.

1. Draw a horizontal, vertical, and diagonal arrow on a grid for an illustrative transformation. Apply it. The horizontal arrow doubles; the vertical arrow remains; the diagonal arrow changes direction. Narration names only what the viewer can observe.
2. Strip away the grid texture and mark the lines that remain fixed. Introduce “eigenvector” for a nonzero arrow that stays on its line and “eigenvalue” for its signed scale factor. Show `Av = λv` as the compact record of the visible transformation.
3. Change the horizontal factor from 2 to 3 and pause for a prediction. Reveal the answer and distinguish a line staying fixed from an arrow necessarily keeping its orientation. A rubber-sheet analogy captures stretching but not every linear transformation.

This is an example, not a canonical three-scene format. A different transformation or audience may need different beats.
